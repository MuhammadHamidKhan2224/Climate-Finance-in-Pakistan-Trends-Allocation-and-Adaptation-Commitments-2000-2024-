What the data show

  1. The workbook records US$21.84 billion in commitments in constant 2024 dollars across 2,479 retained records. This is the recorded portfolio, not all climate investment in Pakistan.
  2. Standard loans account for 84.49% of financial value. Standard grants account for 14.77% of value but 82.09% of records.
  3. The three largest providers supply 64.76% of recorded finance: IDA, the Asian Development Bank, and IBRD. IDA and IBRD are reported as separate providers.
  4. Energy receives 36.90% of financial value. Sector classification describes the reported purpose, not measured environmental performance.
  5. The 2024 total is US$2.29 billion, 7.85% above the previous year in constant dollars. The largest annual amount in this file occurs in 2020 (US$2.41 billion).

8. Data preparation and checks

  • Original file: 2,481 rows and 40 columns, all recipients Pakistan.
  • Excluded 2 exact duplicates across all original columns. Retained 2,479 records. The excluded duplicates total US$9.108 million in constant 2024 dollars.
  • Did not deduplicate on project ID, provider or title. These can appear on several legitimate records.
  • Converted USD-thousand fields to USD by multiplying by 1,000. Used supplied constant 2024 USD for comparisons over time.
  • Verified adaptation + mitigation − overlap equals total finance for every retained record, within US$1.
  • Retained missing sector, instrument and flow categories as “Not reported”. Missing component amounts remain missing in the original-record export.
  • Did not multiply the reported amounts by Climate Share (%) again. Used the supplied climate-finance amount fields directly.

9. Interpretation and the next stage

This is descriptive analysis of reported climate-related development finance commitments. Commitments are promises of funding, not proof of disbursement, completion or climate impact. Provider coverage and reporting methods can change over time. The file includes public providers and private donors; it does not represent all domestic and private climate investment in Pakistan.

Analyse adaptation composition from 2010 onward because adaptation is not separately reported in the earlier records. The grant share for the entire portfolio differs from the adaptation grant share.

After this portfolio analysis, use the separate annual indicators to investigate adaptation commitments per person, adaptation commitments as a percentage of GDP, and the adaptation grant share. With 15 annual adaptation observations, begin with trends, period comparisons and changes in composition. The financial records alone cannot establish effects on emissions or resilience.

Useful research questions: Which providers explain annual peaks? Are loans concentrated in particular sectors? Do adaptation-only and dual-purpose activities have different grant shares? Does an increasing record count coincide with an increasing financial amount?

Source and definitions

Source workbook: Climate Finance Pakistan Funds.xlsx. Calculations use the first sheet, Pakistan records only, with exact duplicate removal documented above.
